**Concept Summary**: A dedicated tool for managing the full lifecycle of tea acquisition — what you've bought, what you have on hand, and where it comes from. The interesting tension: tea enthusiasts build relationships with specific vendors and varietals over time, but the knowledge of what's in the cabinet, what's running low, and which vendor had that great first flush Darjeeling lives entirely in memory.

**Problem Angles**:
- *The repeat buyer who forgets*: Someone who orders from 5+ vendors and can't remember if they already have a Taiwanese oolong from that shop, or whether the last batch from a vendor was good. Purchase history is scattered across email receipts and bank statements.
- *The inventory-blind collector*: A person with 20-40 teas who opens the cabinet and can't gauge what needs restocking vs. what's going stale. The mental load of tracking freshness and quantity limits how much they can enjoy the hobby.
- *The vendor-loyal taster*: Someone who cares deeply about sourcing — single-origin, seasonal harvests, specific farms — but has no structured way to connect a tea they loved back to the vendor, harvest, and purchase date.

**Open Questions**:
- If the primary pain is purchase tracking (what did I buy, from where, when), the product is a glorified receipt log. If it's inventory awareness (what do I have now), it's a living dashboard. These are different products.
- If users already track in spreadsheets or notes apps, the barrier is switching cost and the hook must be dramatically less friction. If they don't track at all, the hook is making the invisible visible.
- If vendor relationships matter (reordering, seasonal drops, loyalty), the product has a commerce angle. If it's purely personal collection management, it stays simple.
