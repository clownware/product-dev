**Problem Statement**: Tea collectors with growing inventories need a way to know what they have, what's aging, and what needs attention because their collection outgrows memory, leading to waste, redundant purchases, and lost connection to trusted vendors.

**Elaboration**: A collector opens their cabinet and faces a wall of tins and bags. They can't remember when they opened the pu-erh, whether the gyokuro is running low, or if they already ordered that second flush Darjeeling from the vendor they trust. Teas go stale unnoticed, duplicates get ordered, and the joy of a curated collection turns into cognitive overhead. The vendor dimension compounds it — they know they loved a specific harvest from a specific farm, but can't trace the thread back when it's time to reorder.

**Scope Boundaries**:
- In scope: Inventory awareness (what's on hand, freshness, quantity), vendor-tea association, reorder context
- Out of scope: Social sharing, marketplace/e-commerce, brewing guides or education
- Adjacent but deferred: Tasting notes, flavor profiling, purchase price tracking

**Assumptions to Validate**:
1. Collectors actually lose track of inventory at a frequency that causes real waste or frustration (vs. enjoying the serendipity of rediscovery)
2. The vendor connection is important enough to track — collectors want to know where a tea came from when deciding to reorder (vs. just buying whatever's available)
3. A dedicated tool provides enough value over a spreadsheet or notes app to justify the switching cost
