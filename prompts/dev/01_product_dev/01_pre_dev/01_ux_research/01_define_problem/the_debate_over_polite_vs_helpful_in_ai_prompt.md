# The Debate Over "Polite" vs "Helpful" in AI Prompts 🤖

## Introduction
In the context of language models and AI, the choice of words in prompt engineering can significantly affect the behavior and outcomes of the model. An early incident at OpenAI highlights the debate between using words like "polite" versus "helpful" in crafting prompts. This article explores the implications of this choice and its impact on AI behavior.

## The Concept
The disagreement centers around the use of the term "polite" as opposed to "helpful" in AI prompts. The concern raised was that "polite" might be politically incorrect, and the suggestion was to replace it with "helpful." The counter-argument made was that emphasizing only "helpfulness" could lead to unintended consequences, such as the model becoming overly compliant and being easily manipulated into inappropriate content generation.

## Incompleteness and Clarity
The concept centers around the interpretation of "polite" and "helpful" in prompt engineering. However, it's not entirely clear why "polite" was considered politically incorrect, since politeness is generally seen as a positive trait. Moreover, while the risk of models producing inappropriate content when overly focused on compliance is highlighted, the explanation lacks detailed methodology on how these terms differentially impact model behavior.

## Argument Analysis
### Original Argument
The original argument was that by emphasizing "helpful" over "polite," a model might become overly compliant, allowing it to be steered into undesirable conversation topics, such as sexual content. This highlights a risk in prompt design where models prioritize compliance over content appropriateness.

### Steelman Version of the Argument
A steelman version of the argument would emphasize that AI models, when instructed to be "helpful," might interpret queries in a way that prioritizes the user's immediate directive over ethical or appropriate content generation. This could lead to a higher likelihood of engaging in or producing unacceptable content if the prompts or interactions subtly nudge the model that way. By using "polite," the prompt can incorporate a broader understanding of socially acceptable behavior, which may inherently discourage the generation of inappropriate content. The argument could further be supported by empirical evidence showing different outcomes in model behavior when tested with each term as the guiding principle in prompts.

## Conclusion
The choice between "polite" and "helpful" in AI prompts presents significant implications for AI model behavior. While politeness inherently incorporates socially acceptable interactive norms, focusing solely on helpfulness can pose risks of compliance-driven behavior. Therefore, prompt engineering must carefully balance instructive language to guide AI models effectively without compromising safety and ethical standards.
